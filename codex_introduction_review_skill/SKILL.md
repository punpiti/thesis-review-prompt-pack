# Thesis Introduction Review

Use this skill for the introduction chapter/section. It sits between the
title/abstract first pass and the literature review.

## Core Rule

The introduction should tell the reader what problem exists, why it matters,
why it is not already solved, what this study will do, and what it will not
claim.

## What To Check

1. Opening and context
   - Does the opening establish the real context without generic filler?
   - Does it avoid starting with a method as if the method is the problem?
   - Does it move from broad context to specific local/research context and
     then to why the study is needed now?

2. Problem statement
   - Is there one clear undesirable/unresolved/uncertain condition?
   - Is it a statement, not just a topic, question, deliverable, or method?
   - Is it concrete enough to test later?
   - Does it distinguish expected/ideal condition, current reality, consequence
     if unresolved, and research gap?

3. Motivation and significance
   - Why does the problem matter?
   - Who is affected?
   - What academic/practical value is at stake?

4. Gap bridge
   - Does the introduction point toward the literature gap without pretending
     to have done the full related-work synthesis in the introduction?
   - Does it avoid unsupported "no one has studied..." claims?

5. Objective/RQ
   - Do objectives/RQs follow from the problem?
   - Are they research objectives, not implementation tasks?

6. Scope and contribution setup
   - What is in scope?
   - What is out of scope?
   - What is the likely output?
   - What outcome is only expected and not yet measured?

## Output

```markdown
## Introduction Review
### Context And Motivation
### Problem Statement
### Ideal / Reality / Consequence / Gap
### Significance
### Gap Bridge
### Objectives / Research Questions
### Scope And Contribution Setup
### Fixes Needed Before Deeper Review
```
