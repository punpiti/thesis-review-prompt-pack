# Thesis Proposal Readiness

Use this skill for early-stage proposal work. It adapts common proposal-writing
heuristics into a review checklist. The goal is to help the student see what is
missing or weak, not to ghostwrite a submission-ready proposal.

## Core Rule

Create review notes first. Do not turn rough input into a polished proposal
unless the user explicitly asks for a revision/drafting pass after review notes
exist.

## Minimum Inputs

At least some of:

- topic or tentative title
- key problem
- field/subfield
- population/context
- intended methodology
- expected output/outcome
- early references or prior-work notes

If these are missing, produce a proposal intake checklist instead of inventing
content.

## What To Check

1. Title direction
   - Is the title concise and specific?
   - Does it name the problem/object, context/population, and key method or
     outcome when appropriate?
   - Provide critique against three possible title styles:
     formal/technical, reader-facing/current, and outcome-oriented.

2. Introduction funnel
   - macro context -> local/specific context -> why this study is needed now
   - avoid generic opening claims that could fit any topic
   - avoid starting with a tool/method before the problem is clear

3. Problem framing
   - ideal or expected condition
   - current reality / pain point
   - consequence if unresolved
   - research gap and why the proposed study is needed
   - gap type: conceptual, methodological, contextual, empirical, data,
     evaluation, implementation, policy/practice

4. Research questions and objectives
   - RQs follow from the problem/gap
   - objectives map one-to-one or clearly to RQs
   - objectives are research objectives, not only activities or deliverables

5. Methodology alignment
   - Quantitative: compare, estimate, test association/effect, predict, model.
   - Qualitative: explore, understand, interpret, synthesize, explain process.
   - Design/R&D: design, build, validate, evaluate performance/usability.
   - Mixed method: show how phases connect and why both are needed.
   - Flag verbs that imply evidence the method cannot produce.

6. Scope and feasibility
   - population/sample/context boundaries
   - inclusion/exclusion criteria if applicable
   - instruments, data source, procedure, and analysis plan
   - ethics/IRB/legal/data permission risks

7. Expected contributions
   - academic contribution: theory, concept, model, method, evidence, dataset,
     measurement, or field-specific knowledge
   - practical/policy contribution: who can use it, how, and under what limits
   - separate direct output from downstream outcome

## Output

```markdown
## Proposal Readiness Review
### Proposal Snapshot
### Title Direction
### Introduction Funnel
### Problem Framing
### Research Gap
### RQ / Objective Alignment
### Methodology Fit
### Scope / Data / Ethics Feasibility
### Expected Contributions
### Missing Inputs
### Minimum Fix Before Proposal Drafting
```

Use cautious language for early ideas: `not clear yet`, `needs evidence`,
`proposal risk`, `requires advisor judgment`, and `needs source verification`.
